Notes on the line get a normal notehead, notes a half step up are an upward pointing triangle above the line, and notes a half step down are a downward facing triangle below the line. Notes get colored by the diminished chord they belong to: D diminished is blue, G diminished is red, and C diminished is yellow. This color concept was invented by Tom Glazebrook and is part of a system called Metaharmony.

Traditional

Diminished Staff

E, G, Bb, and Db/C# are red. C, Eb, Gb/F#, A are yellow. B, D, F, Ab/G# are blue. Keys are defined by what tonic chord feels like home, and each major or minor chord is composed of two colors. The E major chord is E G# B, which are red and blue notes, so if E major is the tonic home chord, the key is a purple key. This means the yellow diminished is the complementary diminished (purple and yellow are complementary colors), having strong harmonic pull back to the purple home chord. Barry Harris came up with "creation theory" which saw major, minor, and dominant chords as coming from two parent diminished chords.

This system makes it a lot easier to see the diminished relationships in functional harmony. In the example below, we're in the key of E. You can get a sense of when there is more tension coming from the complementary yellow diminished chord just by looking at how many yellow notes there are.

Diminished Staff

Here's an example showing the closed position triads in the key of C, and below the dominant chords with blue tension and the minor 6 chords with blue tension. These dominants are what Barry Harris called family because they all come from the same blue and red diminished. And the minor 6 chords are what Barry would call "6 on the 5" of the dominant chords. You can see that each dominant chord is the full blue diminished (B D F Ab) where you lower one of the notes (G7 is made when you lower Ab to G). The minor 6ths are made when you raise one note from the diminished (Dm6 is made when you raise the Ab to an A).

ABC Quick Reference

Notes are letters: C D E F G A B c d e f g a b. Uppercase = low octave, lowercase = high octave. Add commas to go lower (C,), apostrophes to go higher (c').

Accidentals: ^ = sharp, _ = flat, = = natural. Example: ^F _B =E

Durations follow the note: C2 = half note, C/2 = eighth note, C3/2 = dotted quarter. Default duration is set by L: (e.g. L:1/4 = quarter note).

Chords: [CEG] plays notes simultaneously. Rests: z (same duration rules). Barlines: | separates measures.

Header fields (one per line, before the music): L:1/4 default note length · M:4/4 time signature · K:C key · Q:1/4=120 tempo · V:1 treble voice with clef.

Tab string pinning (@): When tab is enabled, append @ and a string number to force a note onto that string (1 = highest string). _E@3 places Eb on string 3. Put duration before @: C/2@4 is an eighth note C on string 4. Without @, strings are assigned automatically.
